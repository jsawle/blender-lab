The parts

  • Magma chamber: molten and part-molten rock, here about 4 to 13 km below sea level (measured under Mount St Helens). Shape simplified.
  • Conduit (main vent): the pipe magma rises through. Drawn wider than real so you can see it.
  • Dike and side vent: magma can break out sideways and erupt on the flank.
  • Layers of lava: thick, sticky lava that flowed a short way and cooled.
  • Layers of ash (tephra): ash and rock fragments blasted out in explosive eruptions.
  • Cooled magma: magma that never erupted and cooled slowly underground into granite-like rock.

Where the magma comes from

Stratovolcanoes grow above subduction zones. The sinking plate releases water about 100 km down, the water makes the hot mantle above it melt, and the magma rises and collects in chambers in the crust. This magma is thick and full of gas, so the eruptions are often explosive, and the volcano builds up steep layers of lava and ash.

Follow the plate down under Guatemala, where Fuego stands, in Subduction Zones Lab.
